Invasive vs Non-Invasive Blood Pressures



Re-examining relationships between invasive and non-invasive blood pressure measurements in critically ill children using big data.


Objectives: Blood pressure is a basic vital sign reflecting the cardiovascular status of the patients that can be sampled either invasively using an arterial line (IABP) or intermittingly using an oscillometric blood pressure cuff (NIBP). Differences and biases between the invasive and cuff measurements have been reported in adults and children and yet, at the bedside, clinicians are often faced with the challenge of assigning a confidence to a specific cuff blood pressure measurement and acting upon the measurement. We hypothesized that big data could be used to quantify the relationship between NIBP and IABP measurements and its dependence on patient characteristics in a manner that may aid bedside decision making.


Design: A retrospective analysis of cuff blood pressure readings with associated concurrent invasive arterial blood pressure measurements (452,728 measurements)


Setting: Critical care unit at The Hospital for Sick Children, Toronto.


Patients: 6,298 patients less than or equal to 18 years of age, hospitalized in a critical care unit with an indwelling arterial line.


Interventions: None.


Measurements and Main Results: Using concurrently measured IABP and NIBP we generated detailed two-dimensional distributions and examine the conditional distributions of invasive arterial blood pressure as a function of the non-invasive systolic, diastolic or mean blood pressure and how they are modified by patient age and sex. For any given combination of patient age and NIBP the expected distribution of IABP readings exhibits both marked variability at the population level, and a bias that depends on the cuff value and age. We have developed an online tool to explore these conditional distributions interactively with the goal of supporting decisions on the need for invasive monitoring in a data-driven manner.


Conclusions: Utilizing a big-data analysis approach and a large physiologic dataset provides clinically applicable insights into the relationship between cuff-based and invasive arterial blood pressure measurements that can help guide assessment at the patient bedside.
